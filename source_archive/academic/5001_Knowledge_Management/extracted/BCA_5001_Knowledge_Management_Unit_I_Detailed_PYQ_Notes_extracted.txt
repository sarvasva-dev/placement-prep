1. Business Intelligence and Business Decisions
Business Intelligence (BI) is a combination of methods, processes, technologies and tools that collect organizational
data, integrate it, analyze it and present useful information for business decisions. BI converts raw data into meaningful
insights.
The main purpose of BI is not merely to create reports. Its real purpose is to help managers understand what is
happening, why it is happening, what may happen next, and what action should be taken.
Primary goals of BI
• Improve decision-making by presenting accurate and timely information.
• Identify business opportunities through trends, patterns and customer behaviour.
• Improve efficiency by locating delays, waste, bottlenecks and unnecessary cost.
• Create competitive advantage through better knowledge of markets and competitors.
• Support a data-driven culture for planning, budgeting and performance measurement.
• Improve customer satisfaction by studying needs, complaints and buying patterns.

2. Modeling the Decision Process
Decision modeling is the systematic representation of a decision problem. It identifies the problem, information,
alternatives, rules, constraints, expected results and method used for selecting an action. A model makes a complex
decision easier to understand and evaluate.
General steps
• Identify the decision or problem.
• Collect relevant data, rules and knowledge.
• Select a suitable model type, such as rational, bounded-rationality, intuitive, creative or participative.
• Build the model by mapping inputs, rules, alternatives and outcomes.
• Validate and test the model against historical data, expert judgement and different scenarios.
• Refine the model when feedback or new data shows that improvement is required.

PYQ: What is modeling decision process? (2024–25)
<b>Answer:</b> Modeling the decision process means representing a decision problem through its inputs, objectives,
alternatives, rules, constraints and expected outcomes. It generally involves identifying the decision, gathering information,
selecting a model, building the model, validating it, testing scenarios and refining it using feedback. Decision modeling helps
managers compare alternatives systematically and choose an action that is consistent with organizational goals.
3. Decision-Making Process
Decision-making is the process of identifying a problem or opportunity, developing possible courses of action, evaluating
them, selecting one alternative, implementing it and learning from the result. It is important in strategic, tactical and
operational management.
Steps of decision-making
Step
Explanation
1. Identify the decision
Recognize that a problem, opportunity or need for improvement exists.
2. Define the problem
Clarify the actual issue, its causes, objectives and constraints.
3. Generate alternatives
Develop possible solutions using brainstorming, expert advice, SWOT analysis or decision trees.
4. Evaluate alternatives
Compare feasibility, cost, risk, impact, time and alignment with goals.

PYQ: Explain the steps involved in decision-making process (2023–24)
<b>Answer:</b> A sound decision-making process begins with identifying the decision and defining the problem or
opportunity. The decision-maker then generates alternatives, evaluates them using cost, risk, feasibility and organizational
objectives, and selects the most appropriate alternative. The selected option is implemented through an action plan,
resource allocation, responsibilities and deadlines. Finally, performance is monitored, feedback is collected and the decision
is adapted when circumstances change. This process makes decisions systematic, measurable and capable of continuous
improvement.

4. Decision Support System (DSS)
A Decision Support System is a computer-based information system that helps managers analyze data, compare
alternatives, use models and make decisions, especially for semi-structured and unstructured problems. DSS supports
human judgement; it does not replace the decision-maker.
DSS components
Component
Function
Data management subsystem
Stores and provides access to internal and external data, databases, data warehouses and
data files.
Model management subsystem
Contains statistical, financial, forecasting, simulation or optimization models.
Knowledge base
Stores rules, facts, procedures or expert knowledge where required.
User interface
Allows managers to enter queries, assumptions and alternatives and view results.
Decision-maker/user
Interprets results and combines system output with human judgement.
Types of DSS
• Data-driven DSS: uses databases and large data sets to generate reports, trends and summaries.
• Model-driven DSS: uses mathematical models, simulations or optimization.
• Communication-driven DSS: supports meetings, collaboration and group decisions.
• Document-driven DSS: searches and analyzes documents, reports, emails and web pages.
• Knowledge-driven DSS: provides recommendations using rules, expert knowledge or AI.
Role of DSS in business
• What-if analysis: examines the effect of changing input variables.
• Goal-seeking analysis: determines input values needed to achieve a target.
• Risk analysis: evaluates possible risks associated with alternatives.
• Model building: represents relationships, assumptions, constraints and variables.
• Graphical analysis: uses graphs to reveal trends, comparisons and patterns quickly.
DSS construction/life cycle
• Problem identification and scope analysis.
• Decision about development method and tools.
• Prototype development using knowledge and requirements.
• Prototype validation and revision.
• Planning for the full-scale system.
• Implementation, maintenance and evaluation.

5. Group Decision Support System (GDSS)
A Group Decision Support System is an interactive, computer-based system that supports several decision-makers
working together to solve semi-structured or unstructured problems. It collects inputs from multiple participants and
provides tools for idea generation, discussion, ranking, voting and documentation.
GDSS meeting structure
Participants use connected computers or devices. A facilitator manages the meeting phases, controls the tools and
displays results. The phases may include problem definition, idea generation, discussion, prioritization, voting, vote
counting, decision recording and follow-up.
Components of GDSS
Component
Details
Hardware
Computers, network equipment, display/projector, audio-video equipment and a suitable meeting
room.
Software tools
Electronic questionnaires, brainstorming, idea organizers, priority-setting, ranking, voting, shared
documents and meeting records.
People
Participants, facilitator, decision-makers, technical support staff and subject experts.
Features
• Ease of use through an interactive interface.
• Improved decision quality by combining diverse viewpoints and information.
• Support for semi-structured and unstructured decisions.
• Specific support for idea generation, discussion, ranking and voting.
• Support for all decision phases: intelligence, design, choice and implementation.
• Anonymous participation can reduce fear of criticism and encourage honest ideas.
• Support for local face-to-face as well as geographically distributed meetings.

PYQ: What is GDSS? Explain its components. (2016)
<b>Answer:</b> GDSS is an interactive computer-based system designed to help a group of decision-makers solve
semi-structured or unstructured problems. Its three major components are hardware, software tools and people. Hardware
includes computers, networking equipment and display systems. Software tools include electronic brainstorming,
questionnaires, idea organization, ranking and voting. People include participants, a trained facilitator and technical support
staff. Together, these components make group meetings more organized, participative and productive.

6. Groupware Technologies
Groupware is software that enables people to communicate, coordinate and collaborate over a network. It supports
shared work even when team members are in different locations. Groupware may support communication, shared
information, joint authoring, shared calendars, workflow and project coordination.
Types of groupware
Type
Meaning
Examples
Synchronous groupware
Supports collaboration at the same time.
Live chat, video conference,
electronic whiteboard, screen
sharing.
Asynchronous groupware
Supports collaboration at different times.
Email, discussion forum, file
sharing, workflow, collaborative
documents.
Common tools
• Email: one-to-one or one-to-many communication and record keeping.
• Discussion forums and bulletin boards: archived messages that can be read and answered later.
• Instant messaging and chat: real-time text communication.
• Screen sharing: remote demonstration, training and troubleshooting.
• Electronic whiteboard: shared drawing and presentation space.
• Video conferencing: real-time audio and video communication.
• Document sharing and collaborative authoring: multiple users access or edit common files.
• Shared calendars: meeting planning and schedule visibility.
• Workflow systems: route tasks and messages through defined stages.
Asynchronous groupware
Asynchronous groupware allows collaboration without requiring all participants to be online at the same time. It is useful
for geographically dispersed teams, academic discussions, project work and organizational memory. Examples include
email, structured messages, workflow systems, discussion boards, file-sharing systems, collaborative writing systems
and knowledge repositories.

Last exam-writing checklist
• Write the complete form before using an abbreviation: BI, DSS, GDSS, EIS, OLAP, OLTP.
• Do not confuse DSS with MIS: DSS emphasizes analysis and managerial judgement, while MIS usually provides
routine structured reports.
• Do not describe groupware as only chat; include document sharing, workflow, calendars and collaboration.
• For GDSS, always mention facilitator, voting, brainstorming and group participation.
• For BI, mention data collection, ETL, storage, analysis and visualization.
• When citing a PYQ year in your notes, write the exact year beside the question/topic.
