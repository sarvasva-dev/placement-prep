3. Business Intelligence
3.1 Meaning and Definition
Business Intelligence (BI) is a combination of methods, processes, architectures and technologies used to collect, integrate,
store, analyse and present business data in a meaningful form. Its purpose is to support better managerial decisions and
improve organizational performance.
In simple words, BI converts raw data into information, information into insight, and insight into action. For example, a
retailer can analyse sales records to discover which products sell most, in which season, in which region and to which
customers.
Exam definition: Business Intelligence is a set of technologies and analytical processes that transform organizational data
into useful information and insights for strategic, tactical and operational decision-making.
3.2 Goals of BI
• Improve decision-making with accurate, timely and relevant information.
• Identify business opportunities through trends, patterns and customer behaviour.
• Improve efficiency by locating waste, bottlenecks and unnecessary costs.

3.4 BI Process: Data to Decision
1. Define: Identify the business question. 2. Collect: Gather data from databases, ERP, CRM, websites, spreadsheets, cloud
systems, IoT devices and APIs. 3. Prepare: Remove duplicates, handle missing values and standardize formats. 4. Store:
Place prepared data in a warehouse or data lake. 5. Analyse: Apply queries, statistics, OLAP, visualization and data mining.
6. Communicate: Present results with charts, dashboards, reports and alerts. 7. Act and monitor: Implement decisions,
track KPIs and learn from feedback.
BI chain: Data → Integration → Storage → Analysis → Visualization → Insight → Decision → Action → Feedback

3.8 Advantages and Limitations
Advantages: improved decisions, faster reporting, better data access, higher productivity, improved data quality, lower costs,
stronger customer understanding and competitive advantage.
Limitations: implementation cost, data-quality dependence, privacy and security risks, integration complexity, need for
trained staff, analytical bias and overdependence on dashboards.
Exam Answer: Business Intelligence
Question: What is Business Intelligence? Explain its components and advantages.
Answer: Business Intelligence transforms raw organizational data into meaningful information and actionable insights. Its
components include data sources, ETL, data warehouse, analytics, reporting and performance management. BI improves
data access, decision quality, productivity and cost control, and helps organizations identify opportunities, understand
customers and compete effectively.
4. Decision-Making Process
4.1 Meaning
Decision-making is the systematic process of identifying a problem or opportunity, developing alternatives, evaluating them
and selecting and implementing an appropriate course of action. Organizational decisions may be strategic, tactical or
operational.
4.2 Steps
Step
Explanation
1. Identify
Recognize that a problem, opportunity or improvement need exists.
2. Define
Clarify the problem, causes, objectives, constraints and scope.
3. Gather information
Collect facts, data, rules, expert opinions and historical records.
4. Generate alternatives
Develop possible solutions through research, consultation or brainstorming.
5. Establish criteria
Choose cost, risk, quality, time and feasibility criteria.
6. Evaluate
Analyse benefits, limitations, resources, risks and outcomes.
7. Select
Choose the most suitable alternative using analysis, judgment or consensus.
8. Implement
Prepare an action plan, allocate resources and assign responsibility.
9. Monitor
Measure outcomes against objectives and KPIs.
10. Learn and adapt
Use feedback to correct the decision and improve future decisions.

6. Group Decision Support System (GDSS)
6.1 Definition
A Group Decision Support System is an interactive, computer-based system designed to help a group of decision-makers
communicate, generate ideas, analyse alternatives, vote and reach a collective decision about a semi-structured or
unstructured problem.
DSS generally supports an individual decision-maker, whereas GDSS supports a team or group. GDSS can be used in an
electronic meeting room or through online collaboration systems.
6.2 Components

8. OLTP, OLAP, Data Warehouse and Data Mart
8.1 OLTP
Online Transaction Processing supports routine daily transactions such as order entry, payment, ticket booking and
attendance recording. It is optimized for fast insert, update and delete operations and current detailed data.
8.2 OLAP
Online Analytical Processing supports multidimensional analysis of historical and integrated data. Its operations include
roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice and pivot across time, product, region and customer.
8.3 Data Warehouse and Data Mart
A data warehouse is a centralized repository of integrated historical data designed for analysis and reporting. A data mart is a
smaller subject-specific part of a warehouse, such as a sales, finance or HR data mart.

9. Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery
9.1 Data Mining
Data mining is the discovery of useful patterns, relationships, trends and anomalies from large datasets using statistical,
computational and machine-learning techniques.
9.2 KDD Process
Knowledge Discovery in Databases is the broader process of obtaining knowledge from data. A common sequence is
selection, cleaning, integration, transformation, data mining, pattern evaluation and knowledge presentation.

10. Important PYQ-Style Questions
1. What is the primary goal of BI? To convert data into useful insights that improve strategic, tactical and operational
decisions.
2. What is GDSS? An interactive system supporting group communication, idea generation, evaluation, voting and collective
decisions.
3. What are the components of GDSS? Hardware, software tools, people and network facilities.
4. What is groupware? Software enabling network-based communication, coordination and collaboration.
5. What is asynchronous groupware? Groupware supporting collaboration at different times, such as email, forums, workflow
and file sharing.
6. What is DSS? An interactive system using data and models to support an individual decision-maker.
7. Differentiate DSS and GDSS. DSS supports an individual; GDSS supports a group and adds collaboration and voting.
8. What is OLAP? Multidimensional analysis of historical and integrated data.

14. Accuracy Notes
• BI includes collection, integration, storage and analysis—not only dashboards.
• DSS supports human judgment and does not replace managers.
• GDSS includes structured idea generation, evaluation, voting and documentation.
• OLTP is optimized for transactions, while OLAP is optimized for analysis.
• A data warehouse is designed for analysis, not routine transactions.
• Data mining is one stage of the broader KDD process.
• Asynchronous collaboration does not require simultaneous online presence.
